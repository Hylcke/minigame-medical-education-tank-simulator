Joakim's Med Ed Tank Simulator

Three short exercises and a timed exam from my session on Russian and Soviet armour. Work out how far away a vehicle is, see how much a rocket drops on the way, and practise aiming in front of something that is moving.

The three exercises

Do them in order. Each one builds on the last.

1Range by mil scale

Look through the binoculars, measure the vehicle in mils and work out the range. There's a practice mode that hides the answer and scores you.

Range = size × 1000 ÷ mils

2Projectile drop

Set the sight to one range and put the target at another. Try the old M72, the newer one and APILAS to see how forgiving each one is.

drop = ½ g t², with t = R ÷ v
Open exercise 2 ›

3Leading a moving target

Choose the speed, angle and range, decide how far in front to aim, then fire and see whether the vehicle drives into the round.

lead = v × t × sin θ

Then take the exam

Five timed levels. You work the numbers out on paper, fire, and get scored on accuracy and speed. Share an exam code and the whole room gets the same numbers.

Go to the exam

Why the order matters

You need a range before you can set the sight. Once the sight is right, a moving vehicle adds the last problem: how far in front to aim.

What's simplified

No wind, no air resistance, flat ground and a vehicle driving in a straight line. Guided weapons like NLAW do all of this for you after a couple of seconds of tracking. Use the numbers to understand the problem, not as firing tables.
